01 Problem Statement

IBM employees spend an estimated 40–60% of their productive workday on coordination overhead: switching between tools, re-reading meeting notes, chasing approvals, manually writing status updates, and searching for institutional knowledge buried across Confluence, Box, Jira, Slack, and email.

There is no single intelligent system that understands what an employee is working on, anticipates what they need next, and acts on their behalf. The result is cognitive overload, missed deadlines, duplicated work, and burnout — at enterprise scale.

IBM Compass is the solution: a proactive AI enterprise work orchestrator that integrates across IBM's tool ecosystem, reasons over an employee's context, and takes decisive action — before being asked.

02 Why This Problem Exists in Large Enterprises

Tool Sprawl

The average IBM employee uses 9–12 enterprise tools daily. No single tool has full context of the employee's work state.

Siloed Data

Knowledge lives in disconnected systems — Confluence, Box, GitHub, Jira, Notes — with no unified knowledge graph or retrieval layer.

Reactive Workflows

All enterprise tools are reactive: they wait for user input. Nobody is watching the whole picture to flag what matters most right now.

Scale of Workforce

With 280,000+ employees across 170 countries, even a 1% productivity gain equates to thousands of reclaimed work-hours per day.

Meeting Overload

Studies show knowledge workers attend 23+ hours of meetings per week, yet fewer than 50% of action items are captured and followed up.

Approval Bottlenecks

Procurement, HR, and project approvals routinely stall for 3–7 days due to no automated tracking or escalation intelligence.

07 Why AI Is Necessary

This problem cannot be solved with traditional automation or dashboards. The challenge is contextual, dynamic, and language-native:

  • Natural language understanding is required to parse meeting transcripts, Slack threads, emails, and documents into structured intent and action items.
  • Reasoning over multi-source context is required to synthesise a Jira board, a client email, and a calendar block into a risk assessment — no rule engine can do this.
  • Generation capability is required to produce high-quality drafts of emails, reports, and Jira tasks that match the user's voice and IBM's tone of communication.
  • Temporal reasoning is required to monitor deadlines, detect drift, and surface blockers that haven't yet been explicitly flagged by any human.
  • Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) over IBM's internal knowledge corpus ensures answers are grounded, cited, and enterprise-accurate — not hallucinated.

15 Pitch for Judges

Every IBM employee starts their day the same way. They open their laptop, and immediately face a wall: 73 unread emails, 12 Slack notifications, a Jira board with 40 open tickets, three meeting invites with no context, and an approval that's been sitting for four days. Before a single line of code is written, a single client is helped, or a single decision is made — the workday has already been consumed by coordination.

This is not a people problem. This is a systems problem. Our enterprise tools were built to record work, not to think about it. They wait to be asked. They do not reason. They do not predict. They do not act.

IBM Compass changes this. Compass is a proactive AI work orchestrator built on watsonx — IBM's own AI platform — that understands your work across every system, anticipates what you need next, and takes action before you ask. It is not a chatbot. It is not a search engine. It is an intelligent colleague that never sleeps, never forgets, and always has context.

Compass reads your calendar, your Jira board, your emails, your Confluence pages, your Slack threads — and every morning it tells you exactly what matters, what is at risk, and what it has already done on your behalf. It drafts your status report. It captures your meeting action items. It flags the sprint that is three days from derailing. It finds the internal playbook you didn't know existed. And it does all of this grounded in IBM's own enterprise knowledge — with citations, with explanations, with full auditability.

The business case is not theoretical. At 100,000 employees saving 12 hours per week, that is 57 million hours returned to IBM per year. At a conservative fully-loaded rate, that is over $2.4 billion in unlocked capacity — not through headcount reduction, but through letting our people do the work they were actually hired to do.

And this is only the internal story. Once deployed and proven inside IBM, Compass becomes a product — IBM watsonx Compass — offered to every Fortune 500 enterprise facing the same crisis. Every bank, every manufacturer, every healthcare system drowning in coordination overhead becomes a customer.

We are not building a productivity feature. We are building the operating system for enterprise work. And IBM — with watsonx, with Granite, with our enterprise relationships, with our 112-year history of running the world's most complex organisations — is the only company in the world positioned to build it credibly, securely, and at scale.
