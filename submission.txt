Solution statement (draft, ~475 words)
Before IBM Consulting begins any client engagement, a lead consultant must judge whether it is actually ready to proceed — stakeholders identified, workshops run, requirements defined, risks surfaced, open questions resolved. Today that judgment is entirely manual: it depends on which consultant runs it and how much time they're given, each engagement starts from zero because lessons from past engagements rarely reach the next one, and under deadline pressure it's the first step consultants compress or skip. A typical review consumes 2–4 days of senior consultant time, and the real cost isn't that time — it's what happens when the review is rushed: gaps like an undefined IAM approach or a missing compliance stakeholder surface after kickoff, where they are far more expensive to fix.

Engagement Ready AI closes this gap. A consultant pastes their raw discovery evidence — workshop minutes, BRDs, emails, transcripts — and receives, in minutes, a scored delivery-readiness verdict with fully explainable reasoning. The four roles this tool models — architect, consultant, partner, and client-facing lead — interact with it at different stages: the consultant submits evidence and makes the first accept/override call; the lead consultant/partner gives final delivery approval; task owners across the team receive and complete assigned remediation work through email and Slack. The AI never acts alone — every irreversible action (sending the delivery package, creating a Jira epic, posting to Slack) requires an explicit human decision first.

Expected outcomes: a consistent, evidence-based readiness gate on every engagement instead of one that depends on who happens to run it; remediation work assigned and tracked automatically instead of manually chased; and a final delivery package (executive summary, client/internal emails, Jira epic, Slack message) generated within the same session a decision is made, rather than drafted from scratch days later.

The productivity gain is a genuine human/AI split of labor, not automation for its own sake: the AI does the reading, scoring, and first-draft writing that consumes the bulk of a consultant's readiness-review time, while the consultant spends their time on judgment — reviewing the AI's reasoning, deciding whether to accept or override it, and approving what actually goes out the door. Every human decision is also written back to IBM Context Studio as an institutional-memory event, so the next assessment for that practice starts smarter than the last — the system compounds in usefulness the more the team uses it.

Time savings were calculated bottom-up, not as a single top-down guess: each manual activity Engagement Ready AI replaces or accelerates (structured evidence review, readiness scoring and reasoning, risk documentation, task assignment, executive summary drafting, delivery emails, Jira epic/story creation) was estimated individually against realistic manual-effort ranges for that specific activity, then summed — yielding roughly 12–18 hours saved per engagement, traceable line-by-line rather than asserted as a headline number.

Technical statement (draft, ~430 words)
Engagement Ready AI is a Next.js 15 (App Router) / React 19 / TypeScript application. The frontend is a set of focused pages — assessment intake, AI findings (verdict, reasoning, and an ask-AI follow-up panel), action center (task tracking and approvals), and engagement summary — built with Tailwind CSS, Zustand for client state, and TanStack Query for server-state caching. The backend is a set of Next.js Route Handlers, one per workflow step, backed by a service layer (one file per external integration) and a single JSON file as the system of record — a deliberate choice over a database, for auditability (the whole engagement state is one diffable file) and zero infrastructure overhead at this stage.

Two distinct AI layers do the work. At runtime, the product runs three sequential IBM watsonx.ai Granite agents — Discovery Consolidator, Delivery Readiness Analyst, Consulting Asset Generator — each strictly typed and schema-validated at the JSON boundary, with an automatic sampling-decode retry if a model response comes back malformed. The second agent is grounded by a live IBM Context Studio query (via MCP, Streamable HTTP) before every assessment, and every human accept/override decision is written back to that same context afterward — a closed institutional-memory loop, not a one-shot lookup. Task assignment and delivery-asset generation are handed to IBM Consulting Advantage through a real Langflow MCP server, with REST and direct Agent-API fallbacks so a single integration outage doesn't stop an assessment.

The other AI layer is how the application itself was built: IBM Bob was the primary development tool throughout, used well beyond autocomplete. It implemented the three-agent pipeline and its retry logic after live-testing against the real watsonx endpoint surfaced a specific silent-failure mode (greedy decoding hitting an early stop token); it wired the MCP client for both Context Studio and the Langflow gateway from scratch, including diagnosing which of several credential paths in the environment were actually live versus dead configuration; and it carried out a full security-hardening pass — after this project's design was reviewed with IBM's Digital AppSec Champion, IBM Bob implemented and live-verified the resulting fixes: an authentication gate, AES-256-GCM encryption at rest with a real tamper test, per-route rate limiting, and explicit timeouts on every external call. Each fix was verified against a real running instance, not just reviewed as a diff.

In short: watsonx.ai Granite, Context Studio, and ICA are the AI that powers the product a consultant uses; IBM Bob is the AI that built, integrated, and hardened that product. Both follow the same principle governing the application itself — AI drafts and recommends, and every consequential action is checked before it ships.
